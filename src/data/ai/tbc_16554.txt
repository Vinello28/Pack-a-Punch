Machine and Deep Learning approaches represent promising solutions for managing cyber risks related to IT and OT systems because they allow complex and concurrent analyses in real-time. However existing methods adopted for detection suffer from several limitations (i.e. too many false positive alarms and so-called concept drift issue). The intrinsic problem is that system dynamics and network traffic change continuously as well cyber risks. Hence there are possible modifications in the distribution of the datasets used for training with respect to those used for testing. In both IT and OT contexts we can confirm that training datasets can easily become obsolete due to changes in workload and/or to new cyber-attacks. The limited available computational resources for (re-)training in real-time scenarios makes it non-trivial to support traffic classification and anomaly detection. Finally the lack of cyber security datasets collected over extended periods makes it impossible to get an industrially acceptable evaluation of the performance of Machine and DL systems when traffic and attacks evolve. This project aims to address main challenges through the selection and experimental analysis of recent continuous learning methods that are compatible with IT and OT system dynamics.
