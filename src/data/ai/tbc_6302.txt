tactile sensors supply useful information during the interaction with an
object that can be used for assessing the stability of a grasp. most of the
previous works on this topic processed tactile readings as signals by
calculating hand-picked features. some of them have processed these readings as
images calculating characteristics on matrix-like sensors. in this work, we
explore how non-matrix sensors (sensors with taxels not arranged exactly in a
matrix) can be processed as tactile images as well. in addition, we prove that
they can be used for predicting grasp stability by training a convolutional
neural network (cnn) with them. we captured over 2500 real three-fingered
grasps on 41 everyday objects to train a cnn that exploited the local
connectivity inherent on the non-matrix tactile sensors, achieving 94.2%
f1-score on predicting stability.
