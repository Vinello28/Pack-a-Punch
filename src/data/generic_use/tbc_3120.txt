It began as an experiment in the small Norwegian town of Insjoen. Once a prosperous paper industry hub  Insjoen fell on hard times as the world transitioned to the digital age. Economic and social depression deepened and then reached its nadir when a teenager shot and killed 22 of his classmates in the local school. This seemed to be the final nail in the town’s coffin. For ANNELIESE BOHM ERICKSEN this presented an opportunity. She had moved to Insjoen from Germany  after marrying the owner of the paper mill. He died shortly before Insjoen’s sequential calamities. However  rather than giving up on her adopted hometown  Anneliese decided to pivot – to embrace  rather than fight  technology. Her son  NOAH  and a few of his peers at Stanford  had developed a new  non-invasive form of AI surveillance technology.
