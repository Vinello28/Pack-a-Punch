A key issue for the development of quantum technologies is the interaction of a quantum system with the external environment. This typically causes decoherence  which has a detrimental effect on the processing/transmission of quantum information and  as such  needs to be suppressed or mitigated. This yet demands detailed knowledge and an effective description of incoherent processes. On the other hand  monitored and/or engineered environments can be fruitfully exploited to carry out useful tasks such as generation of multipartite entanglement and implementation of many-body spin Hamiltonians. THENCE will investigate the open dynamics of generally complex/many-body quantum systems due to the interaction with an external environment  targeting and solving a few but critical open problems in current cutting-edge research. These include: the development of an effective TDDFT method to describe decoherence processes in realistic solid-state models
